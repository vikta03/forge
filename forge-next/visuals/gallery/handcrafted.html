Boring, opened into eight mechanisms.

The source was read through the callers and the data they share. These board fragments illustrate geometric questions, not recommended moves or fresh measured evaluations.

01 / Two opinions per piece

Material + placement

Authored material values and 64-square tables are folded together. Boring keeps their MG/EG totals in the position, so a move can update them by subtraction and addition.

A rook is 500 MG / 640 EG before placement. This is a source constant, not a universal exchange value.

02 / Structure has memory

Pawns + shelter

Neighbours, support, phalanxes, stoppers and pawn spans classify weaknesses and passers. A pawn-key cache reuses them. Shelter additionally checks king position and castling rights.

A knight move need not recompute an unchanged pawn skeleton. The king-shelter cache has extra validity conditions.

03 / Reach is filtered

Piece activity + mobility

Attacks are intersected with a mobility area. Piece-specific mobility tables turn usable reach into MG/EG values; outposts, rook files and bishop restrictions add context.

A knight on d5 is not automatically an outpost: Boring asks about pawn support and whether an enemy pawn can challenge it.

04 / Cooperation is nonlinear

King danger

Safe checks, attacking force, weak ring squares and flank pressure accumulate danger. Defenders and queen absence modify it. Above 100, MG loses danger²/4096; EG loses danger/16.

Illustrative integer arithmetic: danger 160 → MG penalty 6; 320 → 25. Doubling danger roughly quadruples this MG penalty.

05 / Attack is not the whole test

Threats

Protection and double attacks distinguish defended from vulnerable targets. The code separately considers minor/rook threats, hanging pieces, safe pawn attacks and pawn-push threats.

The cheap attacker matters: a pawn attacking a rook is a different feature from a rook merely controlling an empty square.

06 / Promotion is a route

Passed pawns

Rank and file bonuses combine with king distances to the block square, occupancy, control of the promotion path and rooks behind or in front. Pawn-cache candidates feed this later pass.

The same advanced pawn can receive different treatment when its next square is blocked or its route is exposed.

07 / Room is useful only with pieces

Space + coordination

Safe central squares count; safe squares behind friendly pawns can count again. A weight using piece count and blocked pawns is squared. The caller applies space only above a material threshold.

Boring’s space contribution goes into MG only. An empty endgame does not get the same space reward as a crowded middlegame.

08 / Arithmetic meets chess context

Taper + scaling

Phase weights N/B=1, R=2, Q=4, capped at 24. EG scaling depends on material and pawns; ordinary output is damped by the halfmove clock, oriented to the mover and given tempo.

At phase 12 and normal scale, MG and EG have equal weight. This is continuous blending, unlike the Specialists head switch.

Follow the score all the way out.

The ordinary path

Each major term contributes a score pair. Write the total as M for middlegame and E for endgame, from White’s perspective. Non-pawn material determines phase p, capped at 24. The endgame scale s has normal value 64.

v = [M × p + E × (24 − p) × s / 64] / 24
v = v × (100 − halfmove_clock) / 100
result = (White to move ? v : −v) + 20

These are the source equations; C++ integer arithmetic truncates along the calculation. The 20 is Boring’s authored tempo bonus. A diagram of the ordinary route is not a claim that every legal position takes it.

The separate entrance

Before that machinery, Endgames::probe can return a recognized result. The local source includes material-draw detection, a generated king-and-pawn-versus-king bitbase, and guidance for mating a bare king, including bishop and knight.

These special cases bypass the ordinary heuristic sum. They are existing Boring mechanisms being documented; nothing was installed or enabled in your neural engines for this guide.

King shelter also has its own reuse rule: the pawn cache stores it, but king-square and castling-right changes invalidate that part. “Pawn hash hit” does not mean all king-safety arithmetic is blindly reused.

A learned interpretation of a measured board

MyChess2: 106 numbers with names.

The network does not receive the entire handcrafted Boring score. It receives its own feature catalogue: eight global numbers and two identical 49-feature side vocabularies, placed in mover/opponent order. The feature definitions are authored; their influence is learned.

A richer hybrid

netB: the handcrafted terms become measurements.

The ordinary call chain is evaluate_trace → ForgeFactor::score. The trace exposes MG/EG section scores and additional quantities about danger, shelter, safe checks, attackers, flank pressure, hanging targets, passer geometry and other selected signals. The original score itself is also an input. A learned curve can therefore change how a handcrafted measurement is interpreted.

Signed information asks “whose advantage?”

57 values include material and section-score differences, space differences and selected detailed trace differences. The mover’s orientation determines the sign. Magnitude and sign are handled separately when indexing the learned direct and latent tables.

Context asks “under what conditions?”

44 values include totals, magnitudes, phase, rule-50 and scaling information. These help the network treat the same apparent advantage differently in a quiet ending, a tense middlegame or a drawish structure. The route maps use phase and pawn count.

The spatial branch then adds a different kind of information: king-conditioned piece-square embeddings. This is why netB deserves a hybrid drawing with two entrances, rather than an “HCE → neural score” arrow that hides the placement branch.
