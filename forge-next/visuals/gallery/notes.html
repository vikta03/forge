NOTE 01

The zero-parameter trick

Boring’s capacity cube is empty because the chart counts neural coefficients. Its evaluator is anything but empty: lookup tables, caches, geometry, nonlinear king danger and endgame logic all contain design effort. “Zero neural parameters” is a category label, not a verdict on intelligence.

NOTE 02

The word “head” is doing three jobs

A FORGE specialist head is an alternative small evaluator selected by piece count. A transformer attention head is one simultaneous information-mixing channel. A value or policy head is an output branch with a prediction task. Four specialists, twelve attention heads and three output heads are not comparable counts of brains.

NOTE 03

Most of Specialists is not specialist

The three additional heads add 33,216 entries to the single-head design, about 1.04%. The main table is shared. The analogy is four readers using one enormous library—not four copies of the library. Only one reader is called for a position.

NOTE 04

The two meanings of “piece-square table”

Boring’s PSQT is an authored score pair for a piece and square. An NNUE feature table may return hundreds of learned coordinates in a king context; their sum is interpreted by nonlinear layers. Vega and Stockfish also have learned direct PSQT paths. Same label, very different computational objects.

NOTE 05

A memory palace versus a conversation

My interpretation: an incremental net is like maintaining a compact dossier as facts change. A square transformer holds another round of discussion among all 64 squares. Neither metaphor is literal cognition. It highlights the engineering bargain: cheap updates versus repeated context-dependent communication.

NOTE 06

Quiet policy is not a second searcher

FORGE’s query scores eligible quiet moves at a particular move-picker stage. VEGAM’s policy participates as MCTS priors. Better move prediction can save search, but it can also alter reductions and consume time. The same-looking “policy on” switch can therefore mean very different things in two engines.

NOTE 07

The tiny model did not invent all its vocabulary

MyChess2’s 17,923 neural coefficients are supported by a substantial feature extractor. Measuring safe mobility, king escape squares or a bishop-proof passer already embeds chess knowledge. This is a clever division of labour, not cheating and not the same learning problem as square-level representation learning.

NOTE 08

I owe the handcrafted side more respect

A generic box labelled “HCE” hides most of its interesting engineering. Boring’s attack maps are reused across several terms; pawn state has its own cache; danger is nonlinear; MG and EG travel as a pair. These are architectural decisions just as real as a residual connection or a learned embedding.

NOTE 09

A beautiful picture can tell a false story

A hundred wires look technical but may explain less than a labelled bypass. This edition gives arrows a direction, distinguishes stored branches from executed branches and separates exact dimensions from schematic geometry. None of the decorative colours is an activation measurement. Dense connections are bundled rather than pretending that every line is a trained weight.

NOTE 10

The bigger cube can do less work

Stored capacity and per-position cost are different quantities. A large sparse table can touch a few changed rows; a smaller dense transformer can repeatedly transform every square. Dtype, cache behaviour, SIMD, batching, GPU transfers and search integration also matter. Cube volume cannot predict NPS, depth or Elo.

NOTE 11

The boundary question deserves a fair experiment

A capture from 17 pieces to 16 selects a different FORGE head, but it also changes the chess. You cannot diagnose a discontinuity by subtracting the two GUI evaluations alone. Hold other factors under control, inspect head predictions and test whether the difference changes search for the worse. Hard routing is a design tradeoff, not proof of a bug.

NOTE 12

What I would admire in each

Boring: the explicit reasoning vocabulary. MyChess2: compression through useful measurements. netB: nonlinear responses without a large dense tower. FORGE 2: inexpensive state updates. Specialists: added reader capacity at small storage cost. Vega NNUE: wider state and a bypass. VEGAM: keeping square-level context alive. Berserk: the broad-memory/narrow-reader contrast. Stockfish: sophisticated sparse geometry and execution. BT4: deep, repeated square communication. This is appreciation of designs, not a strength ranking.

NOTE 13

Wide160 fills the room that was already wired

The old input connector had 328 sockets: 256 placement, 64 empty and 8 state. Two extra 32-channel perspectives fill all 64 empty sockets. The first reader retains its shape, while the shared table gains 786,432 learned numbers. This is new scratch-trained capacity, not a switch that makes an old checkpoint know more chess.

NOTE 14

Deep32 is a small extra floor, not a second skyscraper

One added 32×32 matrix per specialist barely changes the model’s stored volume. Its repeated arithmetic matters more than its file size. In the existing short match screen it underperformed the reference. That observation belongs beside the attractive diagram; a richer-looking pipeline is not a strength result.

NOTE 15

The cache is a notebook, not the learned brain

J62 FullKey keeps Wide160’s exact weights. It gives remembered search results a stronger identity check: all 64 hash bits, rather than a 16-bit tag after bucket selection. At equal Hash this buys fewer wrong-identity matches at the cost of fewer entries and a measured 1.66% paired median wall-NPS reduction. It does not prove a higher score in games, and equal 64-bit hashes remain possible.

A small translation desk.

HCE
Handcrafted evaluation: explicit rules and numerical formulas judge aspects of a position. Handcrafted input features are related, but a neural model can learn their final influence.
Feature row
An indexed vector retrieved from a learned table. A feature being active means its vector contributes to the sum; it does not mean a particular neuron is “thinking” a named thought.
Accumulator
A running vector sum. Incremental evaluation repairs it after moves instead of rebuilding all unchanged contributions.
Channel / unit
One coordinate of a representation or one hidden-layer output. Its human meaning is not established merely by drawing it.
King bucket
A group of king squares sharing a table context. The exact grouping and board symmetry differ between implementations.
Affine / dense layer
A weighted linear combination, usually plus a bias. It mixes input coordinates into new outputs.
ReLU / clipped ReLU
A nonlinearity that suppresses negative values; clipping additionally caps large positive values. It makes stacked linear transformations more expressive than one linear map.
Residual / bypass
A path carrying information around another calculation. A residual normally adds an update to an incoming representation; a score bypass can feed output directly.
Attention
A learned, position-dependent weighting used to mix token information. It is a mechanism, not automatically a faithful verbal explanation.
Token
A representation unit. In these chess transformers each board square has one token; history supplies channels to those tokens.
WDL / expected score
Predicted win, draw and loss mass. Expected game points are W + ½D. A prediction is not a forced result.
Policy
Scores or probabilities over candidate moves. Its effect depends on search: move ordering in FORGE and priors in VEGAM are different integrations.
Phase / head route
Phase can be blended continuously, as in Boring, or used as input/routing information. Specialists uses hard piece-count bins; those bins are not formal chess phase definitions.
Parameter / operation
A stored learned number versus work performed. A huge table can touch few entries; a smaller dense model can do many operations.

The honest limits of this collection.

The models show source-level organization. They do not map silicon placement, measure per-component latency, reveal neuron semantics, prove the quality of chess understanding or rank playing strength. Small example boards are teaching motifs, not hidden engine experiments. The netB numerical cutaway is explicitly different: it uses an archived measured evaluation with a saved independent replay.

The twelve references also have different counting scopes. The FORGE volumes exclude dormant relation storage but retain reserved-zero weights in the declared head shapes. VEGAM includes default-disabled auxiliary outputs. Stockfish counts logical coefficients rather than SIMD padding; BT4’s number is rounded. The ledger keeps those distinctions visible.

Full retraining provenance and chess strength are separate subjects. Architecture is worth understanding on its own; it is not a substitute for controlled evaluation.
