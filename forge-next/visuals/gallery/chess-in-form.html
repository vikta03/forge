01 · Boring

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01Incremental material + PSQT

Two running totals: middlegame and endgame. Moving a piece updates its contribution.

02Pawn structure, remembered

A pawn-key cache stores structure and attack spans. King shelter also checks king square and castling rights.

03Attack maps are shared

Piece attacks and double attacks feed mobility, threats, king danger and passer safety.

04Several kinds of chess knowledge

Outposts, files, weak pieces, pawn races and space each have their own calculation.

05Two score rails become one

Phase blends MG and EG. Endgame scaling tempers only the endgame contribution.

06Exceptions and search

Recognized endings can bypass the ordinary score. Otherwise apply rule-50 damping, side and tempo.

Start with two opinions

Boring does not treat every piece identically throughout a game. Its material constants are pawn 90/120, knight 345/355, bishop 365/385, rook 500/640 and queen 1010/1200 in internal middlegame/endgame units. Piece-square tables add a second pair of placement adjustments. The position maintains these totals incrementally. These numbers are evaluator units, not a promise that a rook is worth exactly 6.4 pawns in every ending.

A dependency graph, not eight independent judges

Pawn analysis produces attack maps, potential passers and pawn attack spans. The piece pass builds attacks for both colours before king safety, threats and passer evaluation read them. A rook therefore matters in several ways: its own placement, usable mobility, open file, pressure on the king, and influence over a passer’s path. Summing these terms is deliberate overlapping evidence; it is not a disjoint partition of the board.

The ending has a separate entrance

Endgames::probe runs before the ordinary evaluator. This local Boring source includes insufficient-material handling, a generated KPK bitbase and guidance for mating against a bare king, including bishop and knight. These are existing mechanisms being documented, not a proposal to add tablebases to your other engines. The ordinary route also has softer scale factors for opposite-coloured bishops, low pawn counts and small material advantages.

The complete ordinary-score exit

Let M and E be the accumulated white-minus-black MG and EG scores, p the non-pawn phase capped at 24, s the endgame scale with normal value 64, and h the halfmove clock. The code calculates v = [M·p + E·(24−p)·s/64]/24, then v = v·(100−h)/100 using integer arithmetic. Finally it changes to the mover’s perspective and adds TEMPO = 20. Recognized endgames bypass this route. This shows exactly where heuristics, state and orientation meet.

My take: Boring is the most readable personality in this collection. You can point to a preference, find the formula, and argue with it. That clarity is an engineering strength; it does not establish playing strength.

02 · MyChess2

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01The board is measured first

Five concept families are computed for each side. The net receives their measurements, not square tokens.

02106 numbers, with names

8 global values + two 49-value side blocks. Swapping whose turn it is changes the perspective.

03128 learned combinations

The first dense layer combines all 106 inputs; ReLU suppresses negative hidden responses.

04What the features describe

Per side: material 6, pawns 10, activity 13, king state 15, threats 5.

0532 combinations of combinations

The second hidden layer can make one measured advantage depend on several other conditions.

06A probability triplet

Three logits become W/D/L by softmax. Expected points are converted to the scalar used by alpha-beta.

What “handcrafted input” actually means

The author chooses what can be reported: doubled pawns, safe mobility, king escape squares and so on. Training chooses how those reports interact and affect the prediction. A doubled-pawn count is not itself a fixed penalty. A small dense net can learn that one defect matters less when other features compensate. However, distinctions absent from the feature vector cannot be recovered uniquely by the network.

The complete vocabulary at family level

Each 49-value side block has six material features, ten pawn/passers features, thirteen mobility/activity features, fifteen king-state features and five threats. Examples include protected passers, bishop-proof passers, rook open files, enemy-half activity, safe invasion, king-ring attackers, virtual king mobility, shelter, checking pressure, hanging pieces and pawn-push threats. The eight globals are phase, total pawns, opposite-coloured bishops, king distance, rule-50 clock, simple opposite-bishop ending, rook ending, and pawns confined to one wing.

A tiny net with a substantial front end

The selected frozen model is 106→128→32→3, with 17,923 weights and biases and a 71,728-byte file. Computing the feature vector is also work: the tiny parameter count does not include attack generation or chess geometry. The runtime source supports additional variants, but this illustration is the published baseline.nnf, not a later 130-feature model or a policy-augmented checkpoint.

From three outputs to a search score

Softmax normalizes the three logits into W, D and L. The engine uses E = W + D/2, then 187·log(E/(1−E)), with clipping and rounding. This is a mapping from a prediction to the search’s numeric scale. It is not a recovered count of material, and its calibration is not directly interchangeable with another engine’s GUI score.

My take: this is an excellent bridge between interpretable chess measurements and learned evaluation. The price of a concise vocabulary is that two strategically different boards can look identical after being compressed into the same 106 numbers.

03 · FORGE netB

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01The handcrafted front end survives

Boring’s section scores and detailed trace are inputs. Ordinary netB does calculate that chess knowledge.

0257 signed + 44 context features

Each feature selects one of 129 bins in learned tables. Direct tables and latent tables serve different paths.

03King-conditioned placement

32 king buckets × 768 piece-square rows × 16 channels. This branch adds spatial information.

04Direct route plus nonlinear route

8 signed, 8 context and 16 placement channels are pooled. Eight gated products make 40 mixer inputs.

05Routed 40 → 16 mixers

Eight routes are stored; phase and pawn count choose routes for advantage and decisiveness.

06A and C have different jobs

A controls win-versus-loss balance; C controls decisive-versus-draw mass. Combine both to form W/D/L.

Not merely a neural replacement score

In this release, evaluate_trace computes Boring’s handcrafted evaluation and trace. Recognized special endgames return through that earlier route. Ordinary positions then enter ForgeFactor::score with the trace, the Boring score and placement accumulators. The learned evaluator has access both to broad summaries and to details that a single handcrafted scalar would hide.

Why there are two sorts of features

Signed inputs describe an advantage from the mover’s perspective: material differences, MG/EG section differences, space differences and selected detailed trace differences. Context inputs include totals, magnitudes, phase, rule-50, scaling and other background conditions. A feature’s bin selects learned responses; it does not select a named strategic plan. The exported netB-best configuration has 57 signed and 44 context inputs.

A table can be a nonlinear function

A 129-entry response table can give different effects to small and large values of a concept without evaluating a large dense layer. Direct table responses contribute to A and C. Latent tables pool learned eight-dimensional signed and context vectors; the placement sum adds sixteen dimensions. Eight products of tanh(signed) and sigmoid(context) let context gate signed information. The resulting 40-vector enters a 16-unit clipped-ReLU mixer. In this runtime, route A selects that hidden mixer; its two output projections use route A for advantage and route C for decisiveness.

A measured example, already in the release

For the archived crowded-middlegame fixture, direct A/C contributions were −0.50063038 and −0.72753459. The nonlinear branch added +0.19143168 and +0.58465405. Final A = −0.30919870 and C = −0.14288054 produced W/D/L = 0.19656014 / 0.53565949 / 0.26778037, or −27 cp after conversion. These are saved measurements, not a new run. The mixer substantially changes the direct-table story; a list of direct feature contributions is not the whole explanation.

My take: netB is the most explicit hybrid here. It keeps a chess expert’s vocabulary but learns its curves, context and interactions. The architecture makes the handcrafted work visible rather than pretending it vanished.

04 · FORGE 2

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
0132 contexts in one shared table

Each king bucket has 768 piece-square rows. Every row is a learned 128-number vector.

02Two views of the same board

Own and opposing perspectives use the same table. Move updates add and subtract changed rows.

03328 input slots

256 placement channels + 64 reserved zero slots + 8 state values. Grey is inactive storage, not hidden chess knowledge.

04The ordinary evaluator is learned

No live Boring mobility, pawn-penalty or king-danger score is summed into this placement model.

05One 32-unit head

A small integer dense layer turns the accumulated representation into two value logits and a 16-value query.

06A move is matched to a query

Shared from/to/special embeddings produce quiet-move scores. Search decides where policy may influence order.

The representation is not a table of piece values

Every occupied piece-square identity retrieves 128 learned numbers in a king context. The two accumulated sums are clipped and mixed nonlinearly. Two pieces can together activate a unit even when either alone would not. That creates interactions despite the inexpensive summation. It does not justify naming a coordinate “king safety” without controlled evidence.

Why it can be fast

For an ordinary non-king quiet move, unchanged pieces retain their contributions: subtract the old row, add the new row, for each perspective. Captures, promotions, castling and king-context changes require the appropriate additional changes or refreshes. The engine also protects cached head results with position identity and halfmove clock. A table with millions of entries need not be scanned millions of times per position.

The human design did not disappear

Piece identities, canonical orientation, king buckets, state inputs and chess rules remain human choices. The eight state values are halfmove clock, phase, own and enemy castling rights, en-passant square, piece count, check flag and a constant. Phase is input information, not a hand-written material advantage. The 64 relation slots are zero in this placement checkpoint; the dormant relation tensors are not participating knowledge.

Value and policy share a representation

The 32-unit head feeds two value logits and a 16-component policy query. Shared piece/from, piece/to and special-move embedding tables produce candidate vectors; their dot products with the query give policy scores. In this integration policy acts at the quiet stage, normally depth at least four, outside check and with at least two candidates. TT moves, good captures and refutations precede it. This is a different role from a prior guiding every expansion in MCTS.

My take: the central trick is moving work from “re-evaluate the entire board” to “repair a running summary.” Efficiency buys a search budget; whether the compressed summary is good enough remains an empirical question.

05 · FORGE Specialists

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01The big memory is shared

The same 32 × 768 × 128 placement table supplies every specialist. It is not copied four times.

02The two accumulators remain

Two 128-channel perspectives, clipping, 64 zero slots and 8 state inputs match the placement design.

03A deterministic selector

head = floor((piece count − 1) / 8), clamped to 0…3. Kings count. Selection is not learned attention.

04Four stored heads

H0: 2–8 pieces. H1: 9–16. H2: 17–24. H3: 25–32. Each contains its own hidden, value and query weights.

05Only one is evaluated

There is no averaging or voting across the four heads. A boundary capture changes the selected reader.

06Shared move vocabulary

All heads query the same move embeddings. The query is specialist-specific, the move tables are shared.

What specialization actually adds

A shared placement encoder still does most of the representational work. Each selected head has 328×32 hidden weights, 32×2 value weights and 32×16 query weights: 11,072 entries. Adding three extra heads costs 33,216 entries, about 1.04% over the single-head model’s declared placement/head/policy count. It does not multiply the large table or evaluate four networks per node.

The boundary is real; harm is not automatic

Capturing from 17 pieces to 16 switches H2 to H1. The board also changes: a piece has disappeared, the turn changes and possibly the halfmove clock resets. A score difference across that capture cannot by itself identify a bad boundary. Hard routing allows disagreement; establishing an unwanted discontinuity requires controlled diagnostics and search evidence, not just two adjacent GUI scores.

Why a wider reader might help

An ending and a crowded middlegame may need different interpretations of the same accumulated channels. Separate heads can allocate their small capacity differently. That is the intended mechanism, not proof that H0 has learned opposition or H3 understands an attack. The ranges are mechanical piece counts, not clean chess categories: a queen ending and a rook ending can share one head.

What is and is not inside this reference

This plate is the J45 pass-12 Specialists checkpoint used by the existing detailed explorer. Declared participating-path slots total 3,214,720; 1,057,152 dormant relation entries are excluded from that count. Some counted hidden weights connect to zero inputs, and some policy piece codes are unused. Colours illustrate storage and routing, not recorded activations or measured strength.

My take: four specialists sharing one memory is an appealing capacity bargain. The interesting engineering question is coordination at their borders—not whether four is a more impressive number than one.

Counting scope: Declared placement/head/policy tensors. Excludes 1,057,152 dormant relation slots; includes zero-input slots. Four heads are stored, one selected.

06 · FORGE Wide160

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
0132 channels more in every row

Each of 24,576 rows grows from 128 to 160 numbers. The pale upper shelves show the extra capacity, not a separate relation network.

02Both perspectives get wider

Own and opposing sums each carry 160 channels. The same shared table supplies both; it is not duplicated for each side.

03The empty connector is filled

160 + 160 + 8 state = 328. All 320 placement slots now carry learned inputs. No reserved-zero block remains.

04Four readers, still 32 units

H0: 2–8 pieces; H1: 9–16; H2: 17–24; H3: 25–32. Only the selected head runs. Kings count.

05Value and policy share the view

The selected 32 responses feed two value logits and a 16-number move query. These outputs retain their earlier dimensions.

06One shared move vocabulary

From: 768 × 16. To: 768 × 16. Special: 8 × 16. Their sum is matched to the selected query for quiet-move ordering.

Where the new knowledge can fit

Wide160 gives each perspective 25% more coordinates before the small reader. The extra 786,432 coefficients are entirely in placement memory: 24,576 rows × 32 new channels. The four reader matrices are still 328×32 because the wider views fill the 64 previously empty input slots. No handcrafted bishop, pawn or king-safety score is inserted. The model must learn what the additional coordinates represent.

What did not get wider

The selected hidden representation is still 32 numbers. The value branch still predicts advantage and decisiveness, and policy still uses a 16-number query. Consequently this does not remove every bottleneck. A broader input summary can retain distinctions that 128 channels lose; it still has to compress those distinctions into the small reader. That is an architectural opportunity, not a promise of transformer-level understanding.

Training is a new model, not a memory patch

J56/J57 trained Wide160 and Deep32 from scratch on the same 213,899,602-example cache, for twelve passes, using the same bounded experiment recipe. Wide160’s selected checkpoint is pass 11. Near piece-count boundaries, neighbouring value readers also receive weighted training supervision; deployment still selects a single reader. There is no live mixture, vote or soft routing. This overlap supervision differs from the earlier J45 Specialists reference.

What the existing evidence says

Wide160’s selected validation expected-score MAE was 8.6839 percentage points versus Deep32’s 8.8117 in that run. J59’s 100-game 8+0.08 screen against zero-skipping Specialists scored 35 wins, 31 draws and 34 losses: 50.5%. That screen did not establish a strength gain. These are historical measurements with particular checkpoints and conditions, not claims inferred from the larger shape. FullKey later changes search-cache identity, with these same model weights.

My take: Wide160 spends capacity on preserving more of the board before the bottleneck. The pleasing trick is that the connector stays the same size; the extra knowledge lives in the shelves and the formerly empty slots.

Counting scope: 4,001,152 declared coefficient slots; native int8 model 4,001,184 bytes including 32-byte header. No dormant relation tensor. Includes unused piece-code slots.

07 · FORGE Deep32

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01Placement stays 128 channels wide

The same 24,576-row shape as Specialists supplies two 128-channel views. J57 learned new weights from scratch.

0264 inputs remain empty

256 placement + 64 zero + 8 state = 328. The reserved weights exist in the file; the CPU kernel skips their zero products.

03First compression: 328 → 32

Each head first mixes the accumulated inputs into 32 clipped responses. The extra layer cannot restore distinctions already lost here.

04Second interpretation: 32 → 32

A separate learned matrix and another clipped nonlinearity transform those responses. Four matrices are stored; one is used.

05Outputs move to the second layer

Both the two value logits and 16-number query now read the second 32 responses. No skip around that added stage is present.

06Policy tables are still shared

The extra stage can affect quiet ordering through its query. The from/to/special tables keep their dimensions and shared ownership.

Small on disk, real work at every evaluated node

Four 32×32 matrices add only 4,096 coefficients: about 0.13% relative to Specialists’ counted model. But the chosen matrix performs 1,024 extra multiply-accumulate terms plus clipping whenever the head needs recomputing. Most of the big placement memory is accessed sparsely and incrementally. Stored capacity and repeated computation are therefore different budgets; the nearly identical cube volumes do not imply identical speed.

Why the second layer is not redundant

The first hidden stage truncates and clips to 0…127. The added stage mixes that nonlinear representation and clips again. It cannot generally be folded into a single linear matrix. It can recombine detected patterns, but it receives only 32 numbers. If the first stage discards a distinction, a later stage cannot reconstruct it from nothing. This was a test of additional interpretation after compression, not additional spatial relationships before compression.

What the drawing does not imply

A second stage is not a second search, an attention block, or a recursive chess plan. It is one small dense integer transform inside static evaluation. Each specialist has its own added matrix; selection still uses piece count. All four heads can be calculated during training for supervised losses, whereas ordinary engine inference follows one selected path. The highlighted head is illustrative, not a recorded activation.

The experiment was not won by depth

In the shared overnight run, Deep32’s selected validation expected-score MAE was 8.8117 percentage points. Its J59 100-game 8+0.08 screen against zero-skipping Specialists scored 24 wins, 35 draws and 41 losses: 41.5%. This run therefore did not support adopting it over the reference at that time control. A narrow later layer, training dynamics and search interactions remain possible explanations; the geometry alone does not identify the cause.

My take: Deep32 makes the storage-versus-computation distinction unusually vivid. The extra floor is tiny in parameter count, yet every fresh head evaluation has to walk through it.

08 · Vega NNUE

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
0132 × 768 × 256 placement

The runtime header declares 24,576 rows. This is the earlier Vega NNUE, not the VEGAM transformer.

02Two 256-channel perspectives

King-file symmetry mirrors the whole board here. FORGE’s king-bucket folding is a different convention.

03Global state joins the sums

16 global inputs project to 32 channels. Combined head input: 256 + 256 + 32 = 544.

04Eight piece-count readers

Each selected branch is 544 → 32 → 32 → 3. Two nonlinear layers sit after the accumulated representation.

05Learned PSQT bypass

A separate learned table feeds the output without passing through the small hidden layers.

06Three output logits

The bypass adjusts the win/loss logits. Do not mistake its learned PSQT for Boring’s authored table.

Same family, different geometry

The selected nnue_contract.nnue header declares 256 accumulator channels, eight branches, hidden width 32 and global projection width 32. It is a wider, two-hidden-layer NNUE design. Vega mirrors the entire board horizontally for its king-side symmetry; FORGE only folds the king file for bucket selection. These details determine which arrangements share parameters.

Two routes to the answer

The nonlinear branch sees two placement accumulators plus projected state. The learned PSQT branch supplies direct output evidence. Such a bypass gives the network a shorter route for patterns that do not need its full nonlinear computation. Both paths are learned; “piece-square table” names an organization of data, not necessarily a human-written chess opinion.

What the volume includes

The 6,637,624 deployed coefficient slots include placement and bias, the 16→32 global projection, 24,576×8 PSQT entries and eight 544→32→32→3 branches. Training-only factorization and auxiliary heads are excluded after export folding. A model’s training parameterization and its deployed array inventory need not be identical.

My take: this is a useful counterexample to “NNUE is just a giant lookup followed by one dot product.” The reader, the global context, the symmetry and the bypass are all design choices.

Counting scope: Folded runtime weights and biases. Excludes training-only factorization and auxiliary heads; includes PSQT. Header checked directly.

09 · VEGAM

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01History is input, not more squares

120 channels at each of 64 square tokens encode the board/history and state. Projection creates width 384.

02Eight repeated blocks

Each block contains 12 attention heads, relation bias, a residual path and a 384 → 576 → 384 feed-forward path.

03Attention plus learned relations

Each 32-wide head builds a 64 × 64 map. Static bias and adaptive rank-16 relations influence it.

04A whole-position summary

Final token states are averaged, then projected from 384 to 128 for position-level outputs.

05Source and destination meet

64-wide square projections, interaction and global context produce one move-policy logit plus action W/D/L.

06Training outputs versus search use

TF3-Q uses value, policy and action Q. Outcome blending and moves-left adjustment are off in this default.

A different place to spend computation

FORGE pools occupied rows into two sums early. VEGAM keeps a separate 384-number state for every square, then revises those states eight times. A square can receive information from any other square in one attention sublayer. This is a richer route to context, but dense token mixing costs work repeatedly; it does not inherit FORGE’s cheap row-subtraction update.

What a block really contains

This implementation uses pre-normalization. Queries and keys generate compatibility scores, scaled by √32. The block adds a learned static relation bias and adaptive L·diag(g)·Rᵀ bias of rank 16, then softmax and value mixing. The output projection is added through a residual connection. A second normalized path expands 384→576, applies GELU, projects back to 384 and adds another residual. The cutaway below shows these separate parts.

One board judgment and many move judgments

A 128-wide global representation feeds position WDL, outcome and duration heads. The joint move decoder uses source and destination representations, their interaction, global context and promotion/castling embeddings. Each legal move gets policy and action-WDL outputs. An attention head is a parallel mixing channel; an output head is a prediction branch. They are not the same meaning of “head.”

Where those predictions enter chess search

TF3-Q uses inherited corrected Lc0 classic MCTS. Policy provides search priors; the position value is a leaf estimate. Action Q contributes to the unvisited-child initialization in the selected configuration, while visited children use backed-up search information. The trained outcome/duration outputs remain in the checkpoint but their default search adjustments are off. A forward-pass diagram is therefore not a picture of the entire decision process.

My take: VEGAM’s distinctive expense buys repeated, position-dependent communication between squares. The comparison with FORGE is not “many parameters versus few”; it is “what is recomputed, and how much spatial detail stays alive?”

10 · Berserk

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
0116 × 768 feature rows

King-conditioned piece-square storage; dimensions are from checked main-source definitions.

02Two broad accumulators

1,024 channels per perspective. Changed features can be applied lazily or a view can be refreshed.

03Clipping produces sparse inputs

Quantized nonnegative activations feed the head. Vector paths can track nonzero chunks.

04A 16-unit bottleneck

The 2,048-channel representation is reduced to 16, then expanded to 32.

The final learned branch produces one number, not a three-way probability head.

The snapshot matters

The checked source declares 16 king buckets, 1,024-wide accumulators and a 2,048→16→32→1 head. Its older README describes different dimensions. This gallery freezes the observed source shapes rather than silently combining generations. The logical inference inventory is 12,617,297 coefficients, excluding alignment padding.

What surrounds the network

Berserk’s evaluation entry checks material draws, updates or refreshes accumulators, propagates the net, then applies phase-dependent scaling and contempt before clamping. Those are explicit non-neural mechanisms around a learned scalar. A large handcrafted positional evaluator is not implied by that wrapper.

A structural observation

The drawing’s narrow neck is intentional. A wide memory and a small reader can coexist. How much information survives the bottleneck depends on training and feature organization; neither its small width nor its large table alone predicts strength.

My take: an impressive network silhouette need not be deep. Here, the striking contrast is between a broad accumulated state and a tiny computational neck.

11 · Stockfish 19

Open the labelled model at full size ↗
01Three separate feature banks

22,528 piece-square slots; 59,808 threat slots; 4,560 pawn-pair slots. These are feature IDs, not authored bonuses.

02Accumulate, then multiply pairs

Two 1,024-channel views yield a combined 1,024-channel product representation.

03Eight stored branches

The selected branch has 32 then 32 affine outputs; both stages use two activation paths.

04Relations have learned effects

Geometry selects active rows. Their numerical contributions are learned, including the PSQT route.

05Bypass and dual activations

Clipped and squared-clipped responses are concatenated. The output also receives an affine skip contribution.

06The score has a wrapper

PSQT and positional outputs meet optimism/complexity, material and rule-50 adjustments before search uses the result.

Feature engineering is not an old-style bonus

A threat feature records a relation chosen by geometry. It does not say that every such relation deserves a fixed bonus of X centipawns. Learned rows convert those sparse feature IDs into accumulated evidence. This is a third category between Boring’s explicit scoring rules and a square transformer’s learned attention.

Inside one branch

For SFNNv16, 1,024 inputs feed 32 affine outputs. Clipped-ReLU and squared-clipped-ReLU produce 64 responses. Those feed another 32-output affine stage and another pair of activations. The concatenated 128 responses feed the scalar output; two first-stage affine outputs also supply a difference skip. A separate learned PSQT contribution joins it. The dedicated cutaway makes these paths visible.

Human choices outside the learned layers

Stockfish 19’s evaluation entry combines the learned PSQT and positional values, adjusts optimism and evaluation using their disagreement, applies material scaling and linear rule-50 damping, then clamps outside reserved score ranges. This is a neural evaluator inside an engineered search system, not an unconstrained end-to-end move oracle.

Version and scale

The selected reference is the sf_19 release tag, not the older dual-big/small-net layout. The calculated 89,957,768 logical coefficient slots exclude SIMD padding. The common-volume cube measures this stored inventory, not how many entries are touched by a move or how long evaluation takes.

My take: the interesting lesson is how much design can be packed into “NNUE”: richer sparse inputs, cheap updates, product features, multiple activation paths and short output routes. Calling it merely a piece-square net conceals the engineering.

A comparison to keep honest

VEGAM and BT4 both keep square representations alive across repeated mixing. Their normalization, relation generators, embeddings and training histories differ. Shared family resemblance does not make VEGAM a scaled copy of BT4, nor does an equal parameter budget ensure equivalent chess understanding.

A useful caution about pictures

Attention maps show where information is mixed, not a certified explanation of a chosen chess move. An arrow between squares can support many computations. A persuasive story about “the bishop head” needs interventions and counterexamples, not just an attractive heatmap.

My take: the dramatic tower is only half the story. The interesting part is what each floor lets squares communicate. A taller tower cannot substitute for good training, numerical implementation or effective search.
